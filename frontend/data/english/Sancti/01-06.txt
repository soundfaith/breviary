[Lectio4]
A sermon of Saint Leo the Pope  
!Sermon 2 on the Epiphany  
Rejoice in the Lord, most beloved; again I say, rejoice: for after a short interval of time, following the solemnity of Christ’s Nativity, the feast of His manifestation has shone upon us: and He whom the Virgin bore on that day, the world recognized on this. For the Word made flesh so tempered the beginnings of our adoption that the newborn Jesus was manifest to believers and hidden from persecutors. Already then the heavens declared the glory of God, and the sound of truth went forth into all the earth, when to the shepherds the army of Angels appeared announcing the Savior, and the star led the Magi before Him to adore: so that from the rising of the sun to its setting the birth of the true King might shine, when through the Magi the kingdoms of the East learned the truth of events, and the Roman Empire was not ignorant.

[Responsory4]
R. Arise, shine, O Jerusalem, for your light has come:  
* And the glory of the Lord has risen upon you.  
V. And nations shall walk in your light, and kings in the brightness of your rising.  
R. And the glory of the Lord has risen upon you.

[Lectio5]
For even the cruelty of Herod, wishing to extinguish the beginnings of the King suspected by him, unknowingly served this dispensation: so that while intent on a savage crime, he pursued the Child unknown to him by indiscriminate slaughter of infants, and everywhere the more notable fame of the heavenly‑announced birth of the Lord was spoken; made more ready to be told both by the novelty of the heavenly sign and by the impiety of the most bloodthirsty persecutor. Then also the Savior was brought into Egypt, so that a nation given to ancient errors might already be marked for near salvation by hidden grace: and that which had not yet cast superstition from its heart might already receive truth in its hospitality.

[Lectio6]
Let us then recognize, most beloved, in the Magi who adored Christ, the first fruits of our calling and faith; and with rejoicing hearts let us celebrate the beginnings of blessed hope. From that moment we began to enter into the eternal inheritance; from that moment the hidden mysteries of Scripture speaking of Christ were opened to us, and the truth which the blindness of the Jews does not receive brought its light to all nations. Therefore let this most holy day be honored by us, on which the Author of our salvation appeared: and Him whom the Magi worshiped as an infant in the manger, let us adore as almighty in heaven. And as they offered mystical kinds of gifts from their treasures to the Lord, so let us offer from our hearts what is worthy of God.

A homily of Saint Gregory the Pope  
!Homily 10 on the Gospel  
As you heard in the Gospel reading, dearest brothers, when the King of heaven was born, the king of earth was troubled: for earthly exaltation is confounded when heavenly majesty is revealed. But we must ask what it means that, when the Redeemer was born, an Angel appeared to the shepherds in Judea, but to lead the Magi from the East to adore Him, not an Angel but a star guided them. For to the Jews, as using reason, a rational creature — that is, an Angel — ought to preach; but the Gentiles, who did not use reason, were led to know the Lord not by voice but by signs. Hence Paul also says: Prophecies are given to the faithful, not to unbelievers; but signs to unbelievers, not to the faithful. For to the Jews prophecies were given as to the faithful, not to unbelievers; and to the Gentiles signs were given as to unbelievers, not to the faithful.

[Responsory7]

[Lectio8]
And note that our Redeemer, when He was already of perfect age, was preached to the Gentiles by the Apostles, but as a little Child, not yet speaking by human voice, He was announced to the Gentiles by a star: for the order of reason required that the Lord already speaking should be made known to us by speaking preachers, and the Lord not yet speaking should be preached by mute elements. But in all the signs shown either at the Lord’s birth or at His death, we must consider how great was the hardness in the hearts of certain Jews, who did not recognize Him either by the gift of prophecy or by miracles.

[Lectio9]
All the elements bore witness that their Creator had come. To speak of them in human terms: the heavens recognized Him, for they immediately sent forth a star. The sea recognized Him, for it offered itself to be trodden under His feet. The earth recognized Him, for it trembled when He died. The sun recognized Him, for it hid its rays of light. The rocks and walls recognized Him, for they were split at the time of His death. Hell recognized Him, for it gave back the dead whom it held. And yet Him whom all the insensible elements felt to be Lord, the hearts of unbelieving Jews do not recognize as God, and harder than stones, they refuse to be broken unto repentance.
