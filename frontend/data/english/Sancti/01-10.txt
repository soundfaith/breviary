[Lectio4]
A sermon of Saint Maximus the Bishop  
!Homily 1 on the Epiphany  
In this celebration, most beloved, as we are instructed by the account of paternal tradition, we must rejoice in a manifold festivity. For they say that today our Lord Christ was either adored by the Gentiles under the guidance of a star; or, invited to a wedding, He changed water into wine; or, having received baptism from John, He consecrated the waters of the Jordan and at the same time purified His Baptist.

[Lectio5]
But what was done on this day in particular is known by Him who did it; we, however, must believe and not doubt that whatever it was, it was done for us. For because the Chaldeans, incited by the rays of a more brilliant star, adored Him, hope was given to the Gentiles of adoring the true God. Because the waters were changed into wine by a new order, the sacrament of the new cup was pre‑tasted for us. And because the Lamb of God was baptized, the saving gift of regenerating baptism was dedicated for us.

[Lectio6]
Therefore, brothers, we must with all devotion celebrate also this birthday of His powers, for the honor of our Savior, whose Nativity we have recently completed with due exultation. And how rightly these three mysteries are proclaimed to us as done in one day, we who confess the ineffable mystery of the Trinity under the one name of God! Through these miracles Christ the Lord, our Redeemer, wished to reveal Himself to the eyes of mortals, so that His invisible divinity, which lay hidden in man, might not lie hidden in His works.

[Lectio7]
A reading from the holy Gospel according to Matthew  
!Matt 2:1-12  
When Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of King Herod, behold, Magi from the East came to Jerusalem, saying: Where is He who is born King of the Jews? And the rest.  
_  
A homily of Saint Jerome the Priest  
!Book 1 Commentary on Matthew 2  
For we have seen His star in the East. To the confusion of the Jews, so that they might learn the Nativity of Christ from the Gentiles, a star rises in the East; which, being foretold, Balaam — whose successors they were — had known by prophecy. Read the Book of Numbers. The Magi are brought by the indication of the star into Judea, so that the priests, being questioned by the Magi where Christ was to be born, might become inexcusable concerning His coming.

[Lectio8]
But they said to him: In Bethlehem of Judea. This is an error of the scribes. For we think that the Evangelist first wrote, as we read in the Hebrew itself, “Judah,” not “Judea.” For what Bethlehem is there of other nations, that to distinguish it, “of Judea” should be added here? But “Judah” is written because there is another Bethlehem in Galilee. Read the Book of Joshua the son of Nun. Finally, even in the testimony taken from the prophecy of Micah, it is written thus: And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah.

[Lectio9]
And opening their treasures, they offered Him gifts — gold, incense, and myrrh. Most beautifully the sacraments of the gifts are summed up by Juvencus the Priest in one verse: “Incense, gold, and myrrh they bring — gifts for King, man, and God.” And having received a warning in a dream not to return to Herod, they went back to their own region by another way. Those who had offered gifts to the Lord consequently receive a response, not by an Angel, but by the Lord Himself: so that the privilege of Joseph’s merits might be shown. They return by another way, because they were not to be mingled with the unbelief of the Jews.
