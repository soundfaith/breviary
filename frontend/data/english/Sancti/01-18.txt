[Lectio4]
A sermon of Saint Leo the Pope  
!Sermon 1 on Saints Peter and Paul, before the middle  
When the twelve Apostles, having received through the Holy Spirit the gift of speaking all languages, had undertaken to instruct the world with the Gospel, dividing among themselves the parts of the earth, the most blessed Peter, prince of the apostolic order, was destined for the citadel of the Roman Empire, so that the light of truth, which was revealed for the salvation of all nations, might more effectively pour itself from that very head through the whole body of the world. For what nation’s men were not then in this city? Or what peoples anywhere were ignorant of what Rome had learned?

[Responsory4]
@Sancti/02-22

[Lectio5]
Here the opinions of philosophy were to be trampled down, here the vanities of earthly wisdom dissolved, here the worship of demons confuted, here the impiety of all sacrileges destroyed — where with most diligent superstition was gathered whatever had been established anywhere by vain errors. To this city, therefore, you, most blessed Apostle Peter, do not fear to come; and with Paul the Apostle, your companion in glory, still occupied with the ordering of other churches, you enter this forest of raging beasts and this ocean of most turbulent depth, more steadfast than when you walked upon the sea.

[Responsory5]
@Sancti/02-22

[Lectio6]
Already you had instructed the peoples who had believed from the circumcision; already you had founded the Church of Antioch, where first the dignity of the Christian name arose; already you had filled Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia with the laws of evangelical preaching; and neither doubtful of the progress of your work nor ignorant of the span of your age, you were bringing the trophy of the Cross of Christ to the Roman citadels, where divine preordinations preceded you — both the honor of power and the glory of passion.

!Matt 16:13-19  
At that time: Jesus came into the parts of Caesarea Philippi, and asked His disciples, saying: Whom do men say that the Son of Man is? And the rest.  
_  
A homily of Saint Hilary the Bishop  
!Commentary on Matthew, chapter 16, after the beginning  
The Lord asks the disciples whom men say that He is, adding “the Son of Man.” For this is the rule of confession to be held: that just as we remember Him as the Son of God, so also as the Son of Man; because either without the other gives no hope for salvation. Therefore, after the various opinions of men about Him were stated, He asks what they themselves think of Him. Peter answers: “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” But Peter had weighed the conditions of the question. For the Lord had said: “Whom do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?” And certainly the contemplation of the Son of Man presented the body. But by adding “Whom do men say that I am,” He signified that besides what was seen in Him, something else was to be understood: for He was the Son of Man. What therefore was the judgment He desired concerning Himself? Not what He Himself confessed about Himself; but what was hidden was being asked, into which the faith of believers ought to extend.

[Lectio8]
And plainly a worthy reward followed Peter’s confession, because he had seen the Son of God in a man. Blessed is he who is praised for having extended beyond human eyes and having seen: not contemplating what was of flesh and blood, but beholding the Son of God by the revelation of the heavenly Father; and judged worthy to be the first to recognize what in Christ was of God. O happy foundation of the Church in the naming of a new name, and the rock worthy of that building, which would dissolve the laws of hell, the gates of Tartarus, and all the bars of death! O blessed gatekeeper of heaven, to whose judgment the keys of the eternal entrance are handed, whose earthly judgment has authority already decided in heaven!
