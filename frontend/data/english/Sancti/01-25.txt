[Lectio4]
A sermon of Saint Augustine the Bishop  
!Sermon 14 on the Saints  
Today this reading from the Acts of the Apostles was proclaimed, where Paul the Apostle, from persecutor of Christians, became a herald of Christ. For Christ cast down the persecutor, that He might make him a teacher of the Church: striking him and healing him; killing and giving life; the Lamb slain by wolves, and making lambs out of wolves. For in that noble prophecy, when Jacob the Patriarch blessed his sons (touching the present, foreseeing the future), it had been foretold what happened in Paul. For Paul, as he himself testifies, was of the tribe of Benjamin. And when Jacob, blessing his sons, came to bless Benjamin, he said of him: Benjamin is a ravening wolf.

[Lectio5]
What then? A ravening wolf forever? Far from it; but one who in the morning devours the prey, and in the evening divides the spoils. This was fulfilled in the Apostle Paul, because it had been said of him. Now, if you please, let us hear him devouring in the morning, dividing spoils in the evening. Morning and evening are set for “before and after.” So let us take it thus: First he will devour, afterwards he will divide the spoils. Consider the devourer: Saul, he says, having received letters from the chief priests, went, that wherever he found Christians, he might drag them to the priests to be punished.

[Lectio6]
He went breathing and panting slaughter: this is devouring in the morning. For when Stephen the first Martyr was stoned for the name of Christ, Saul was clearly present; and he was so present to those who stoned him that it did not suffice him to stone with his own hands. That he might be in the hands of all who stoned, he himself kept the garments of all; raging more by helping all than by stoning with his own hands. We have heard him devouring in the morning: let us see how in the evening he divides the spoils. Cast down by the voice of Christ from heaven, and receiving from above the prohibition of raging further, he fell on his face, first to be cast down, afterwards to be raised; first to be struck, afterwards to be healed.
