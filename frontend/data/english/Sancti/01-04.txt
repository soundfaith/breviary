[Lectio4]
A sermon of Saint Augustine the Bishop  
!Sermon 1 on the Innocents  
At the birth of the Lord, mourning began—not in heaven, but on earth: lamentation was proclaimed to mothers, exultation to Angels, and passage to infants. God is He who was born; those Innocents are owed as victims to Him who came to condemn the wickedness of the world. Lambs must be sacrificed, because He who was to be crucified is the Lamb who takes away the sins of the world. But the mothers, as sheep, wail because they lose their lambs who bleat without voice. A great martyrdom, a cruel spectacle! The sword is drawn, and no cause intervenes: only envy hisses, though He who is born does violence to no one. But we see the mothers as sheep, who mourn over their lambs: A voice was heard in Rama, lamentation and great weeping. They are pledges, not entrusted but created; not deposited, but offered.

[Lectio5]
Nature itself is witness, which fought in the Martyrs when the tyrant hurled the infants. The mother tore her hair, losing the ornament of her head. In how many ways she tried to hide the child, and the little one himself revealed her! He knew not how to be silent, for he had not yet learned to fear. The mother fought, and the executioner: he dragged, she held. To the executioner the mother cried: Why do you separate from me him whom I bore from myself? The womb bore him, he did not remain, and while he lived, he twisted my breasts in vain with milk. Carefully I carried him, whom I now see hurled by your cruel hand. My womb has just poured him forth, and you dash him to the ground.

[Lectio6]
Another mother cried out when the collector‑robber did not compel the mother to be slain together with the child: Why do you leave me empty? If there is guilt, it is mine; if there is no crime, join me in death and free the mother. Another said: What do you seek? You seek one, and you kill many, and you cannot reach the one who alone is sought. Another cried out in turn: Come now, come, Savior of the world, how long will You be sought? You fear no one: let the soldier see You, and let him not kill our infants. The lamentation of the mothers was mingled, and the offering of the little ones passed up to heaven.

[Lectio7]
A reading from the holy Gospel according to Matthew  
!Matt 2:13-18  
At that time: The Angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph, saying: Arise, and take the Child and His Mother, and flee into Egypt, and be there until I tell you. And the rest.  
_  
A homily of Saint John Chrysostom  
!From Homily 8 on Matthew, before the middle  
When Joseph heard these things, he was by no means scandalized, nor did he say: This matter is uncertain and altogether doubtful. You said a little while ago that He will save His people, and now He cannot deliver Himself from dangers: but flight is necessary for us, and exile, and a long journey. These things are altogether contrary to the promises. But he opposes nothing of this: for he was a faithful man; nor does he curiously inquire the time of return, which indeed was not clearly expressed by the Angel, but: Be there, he says, until I tell you.

[Lectio8]
Yet he was not made sluggish by these things, but obeyed willingly and believed, enduring all tribulations with joy. Indeed, the merciful God mingled certain joys with sorrowful things. For He surely does this in all the saints, whom He allows neither tribulations nor joys to be continuous: but He has woven the life of the just with a wonderful variety, now from adversities, now from prosperities. Consider that He did the same here.

[Lectio9]
For when holy Joseph saw the Virgin pregnant, he fell into great confusion: but suddenly the Angel appeared, extinguishing fear entirely. Then seeing the Child born, he was filled with great exultation: but again to this joy succeeded great peril, when the whole city was troubled, the king raged, and sought the Child to kill Him. But to this sorrow another joy followed—the appearance of the star and the adoration of the Magi. After this joy again came peril, and again fear. Herod seeks the Child’s life, and it is necessary to flee and to go into distant lands.
